## 1. The caregiving problem in numbers

### Scale and load
- **63 million US adults (nearly 1 in 4) are family caregivers**, up 20M since 2015. AARP/NAC *Caregiving in the U.S. 2025* (Jul 2025). https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html
- **27 hrs/week on average**. Nearly 1 in 4 caregivers give **40+ hrs/week**, and one-third have been caregiving for 5+ years. Same AARP 2025 report. (The 27-hr average comes via a secondary summary: https://zencaregiving.org/caregiving-statistics-us-2025/ **[WEAK: verify in the full report]**)
- **$600B/yr**: the value of unpaid family care (38M caregivers × 18 hrs/wk × $16.59/hr, 2021 data). AARP *Valuing the Invaluable 2023*. https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2023-03-08-new-report-highlights-increasing-cost-of-family-caregiving-in-the-us
- **6 in 10 caregivers also have a job**, and about half report work disruptions (late arrivals, leaving early). **About $7,200/yr out of pocket.** Nearly half have had at least one major financial impact. AARP 2025 (link above).
- **Medication management is now nursing work.** More than half of caregivers handle medical/nursing tasks such as injections, wound care, and meds, but only about 20% got training for them. AARP 2025.
- **Sandwich generation:** nearly 1 in 3 caregivers also raise kids under 18. That rises to **47% of caregivers under 50**, **43% of Latino caregivers**, and 36% of Black caregivers. AARP 2025.
- **Isolation:** nearly 1 in 4 caregivers feel socially isolated, and 1 in 5 rate their own health fair or poor. AARP 2025.

### Who carries the load (siblings)
- **83% of caregivers have siblings, but only 1 in 10 say the work is shared equally.** In 40% of cases siblings give no help at all. Another 41% get some help but remain the primary caregiver. Northwestern Mutual C.A.R.E. Study (2019). https://news.northwesternmutual.com/2019-care-study **[industry survey]**
- One sibling carries the bulk of a parent's care in **43%** of US families. Home Instead survey, cited via ElderLawAnswers. https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/family-caregivers-in-the-u-s-divided-by-5741816/ **[WEAK: secondary]**
- Daughters give about twice as much parent care as sons, and sons cut back when they have a sister. Grigoryeva, Princeton (2014). https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/03/140318093401.htm

### Medication non-adherence
- **20–30% of prescriptions are never filled, and about 50% of chronic-disease medications are not taken as prescribed.** Direct costs are an estimated **$100–300B/yr**. CDC Grand Rounds, MMWR (2017). https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/54287
- Up to **10% of hospital admissions** and **23% of nursing-home admissions** are attributed to non-adherence. This is widely cited from Osterberg & Blaschke, NEJM 2005, via secondary sources. https://www.ecri.org/search-results/member-preview/ccrm/pages/iiccrm050517_why/ **[WEAK: old, secondary]**
- **"125,000 deaths/yr": DO NOT USE.** Katrina Firlik, MD traced the figure back through the literature. The earliest cited source (CDC NVSR Vol 50 No 15) never mentions non-adherence, and non-adherence can't legally be listed as a cause of death. KevinMD (2022). https://www.kevinmd.com/2022/12/the-unstoppable-momentum-of-a-medical-fact-with-no-source.html. If a judge brings it up, saying we checked and dropped it builds credibility.

### Medication errors at home: the double dose
- **Adults 65+ account for 43.6% of hospitalizations for outpatient adverse drug events.** Anticoagulants, diabetes drugs, and opioids are implicated in about 60% of these ED visits. Shehab et al., JAMA (2016), CDC NEISS-CADES 2013–14. https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/us-emergency-department-visits-outpatient-adverse-drug-events-2013-2014
- **Out-of-hospital medication errors with serious outcomes doubled (rate +100%) from 2000 to 2012**: 67,603 cases and 414 deaths. One-third led to hospital admission. "Inadvertently taking/giving the medication twice" was among the most common error types. Hodges, Spiller et al., Clinical Toxicology (2017), NPDS data. https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/non-health-care-facility-medication-errors-resulting-serious-medical-outcomes and https://www.kaxe.org/2017-07-12/more-people-are-making-mistakes-with-medicines-at-home
- **Among adults 65+ with unintentional pharmaceutical exposures, 30% were a double dose and 20% were more than a double dose.** 76% of the errors were made by the patients themselves. Rietjens et al., Clinical Toxicology (2022), Dutch Poisons Information Center, n=1,051. https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/poisoning-in-older-adults-characterization-of-exposures-reported-/ (Dutch data, but the strongest double-dose figure we found.)
- NYC Poison Control: the most common medication error is a dose **inadvertently given or taken twice**. NYC DOHMH poisoning report (2018). https://home4.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/survey/2018poisoning.pdf
- Poison-center summaries note that double dosing **"often resulted from multiple caregivers giving medication."** https://www.bcpharmacy.ca/tablet/winter-19/medication-errors-patients-and-caregivers **[secondary]**. This is exactly the failure that happens when siblings share care and there is no shared state.

### Polypharmacy and cognition
- About **39% of adults 65+ take 5 or more prescription drugs** (NHANES, 1999–2012 trend 24%→39%). A 2024 analysis puts it at **50.9%**. PMC review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10337167/ and Trilliant Health (2024) https://www.trillianthealth.com/market-research/studies/high-polypharmacy-rates-underscore-pharmaceutical-reliance **[Trilliant = industry]**
- **10% of Americans 65+ have dementia and 22% have mild cognitive impairment.** MCI burden is higher among Hispanic older adults. Manly et al., JAMA Neurology (2022). https://news.umich.edu/one-in-10-older-americans-has-dementia

## 2. Why current solutions fail

**1. The elder must use the app.** **79% of adults 65+ own a smartphone** (Pew, 2024; https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/), so 1 in 5 don't. Owning a phone also isn't the same as reliably confirming doses in an app, especially when 32% of people 65+ have MCI or dementia (Manly 2022). Medisafe's caregiver alert ("Medfriend") requires the elder to run the app and the Medfriend to install it and create an account. https://medisafe.com/faq/what-is-a-medfriend

**2. Caregivers don't adopt the tools.** **71% of caregivers say they want caregiving tech, but only 7% use it.** Project Catalyst/HITLAB (2016). https://healthjournalism.org/blog/2016/06/study-caregivers-want-tech-support-but-adoption-rates-low/ **[WEAK: 2016, the best widely cited figure]**. MIT AgeLab (*Innovation in Aging*, 2025, n=192) found that caregivers under high strain, with lower income, or caring for someone with memory problems adopt fewer technologies. These are the families that most need them. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12762550/. People who stopped using tools cite confusing interfaces, technical failures, and **uncertainty about data accuracy** (Frontiers in Digital Health, 2025). https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2025.1587162/pdf

**3. Voice is the elder-native interface.** Among adults 50+, voice assistants are the top form of AI use, at **51% using or interested**. AI use overall rose from 18% (2024) to 30% (2025). AARP Tech Trends 2025. https://aarp.org/pri/topics/technology/internet-media-devices/2025-technology-trends-older-adults. Joy Calls and Meela grew by working on **any phone, including landlines**. A study of older adults with MCI/dementia found they rejected features that felt like monitoring and accepted ones that supported autonomy (JMIR mHealth, 2020). https://mhealth.jmir.org/2020/7/e16928

**4. One-size apps don't fit any family.** Every product below ships the same screens to every family. None of them adapts to who speaks Spanish, who is on duty this week, or which pill is the blue one.

**Positioning: what's new.** Each piece exists on its own: companion calls (Meela, Joy), med alerts (Medisafe, Hero), coordination feeds (Lotsa, Carely), and app generation (Lovable). **Nobody joins them into one live system that is generated for a single group:**
1. **The least-technical member is a first-class participant over a plain phone call.** Her voice is the input, not a guest view.
2. **Every member gets an interface generated for their role and language** from the family's own description.
3. **Deterministic safety in a loop with no smartphone needed.** Double-dose blocks and missed-dose escalation by phone are handled by rules, not by LLM judgment.
4. **Shared memory with provenance** ("who said the blue pill is 5 mg, and when"). This addresses the "uncertainty about data accuracy" that caregivers give for abandoning tools.
5. **The same engine serves any small group** (family, city block). That makes it a platform rather than a vertical app.

## 4. The blackout village

- **About 500 New Yorkers die each year in warm months from heat** (489/yr heat-exacerbated, 2014–2023). Heat-stress deaths averaged 7/yr (2016–2025), but **2025 had 21**, 19 of them in one 4-day June heat wave. NYC DOHMH Heat-Related Mortality Report (2025/26). https://a816-dohbesp.nyc.gov/INDICATORPUBLIC/data-features/heat-report and press: https://citylimits.org/at-least-21-new-yorkers-died-from-heat-stress-last-year-city-health-dept-says/
- **Who dies:** people 60+ have the highest rates. Black New Yorkers die at 3× and Latino New Yorkers at 2× the rate of white New Yorkers, concentrated in Brooklyn and the Bronx. **88% had a chronic condition.** Among home exposures with AC data, **none had or were using AC**. About 493,000 renter households have AC but don't run it because of cost. (Same DOHMH sources.)
- **Isolation kills:** in the 1995 Chicago heat wave, living alone, not leaving home daily, and being bedbound raised the risk of death. Social contacts and AC access were protective. Semenza et al., NEJM (1996). https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5226a2.htm
- **2006 Queens blackout:** 8 days (Jul 17–25) during a heat wave, about 174,000 people affected. **Con Ed first estimated 1,600 "customers," then revised tenfold**, because one apartment building counts as one customer. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Queens_blackout **[Wikipedia; cite the primary NYT/state PSC report if putting it on a slide]**. The lesson: the utility can't see people.
- **July 2019:** a Midtown/UWS blackout hit 73,000 customers (Jul 13). https://www.wusf.org/2019-07-13/utility-says-power-restored-in-new-york-city-after-outage-hits-73-000-customers. A week later, at 98°F, **Con Ed deliberately cut power to 33,000 customers in SE Brooklyn** (Canarsie, Flatlands), neighborhoods scoring 4/5 on the city's Heat Vulnerability Index. Con Ed said it was unaware of the index. https://grist.org/article/during-deadly-heat-wave-new-york-utility-cut-power-to-high-risk-neighborhoods/
- **Con Edison Life-Support Equipment program:** customers using home ventilators, dialysis, or oxygen concentrators can register for outage alerts and wellness checks. Enrollment is opt-in and per account. https://www.coned.com/en/about-us/media-center/news/2023/06-05/using-life-support-equipment-con-edison-needs-to-know
- **NYC Advance Warning System** sends alerts to about 1,900 *service-provider organizations*, not to individuals. **Notify NYC** sends zip-code alerts by text, email, and phone. https://www.nyc.gov/site/em/ready/disabilities-access-functional-needs.page and https://www.gothamgazette.com/city/8800-7-years-after-sandy-part-iii-is-the-city-s-emergency-responsiveness-ready-for-whatever-comes-our-way. Both are one-way broadcasts, so nobody tracks whether Mrs. Alvarez on the 4th floor is okay.
- **Mutual aid runs on spreadsheets.** In the 2025 LA fires, the Mutual Aid LA Network's "famous spreadsheet" was the de facto resource database, and it took in $785,929 in January alone. https://mutualaidla.org/updates-and-transparency/2025-fire-transparency-report/. A Cornell student turned that sheet into fireaid.info: https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2025/01/student-spins-spreadsheet-online-hub-wildfire-relief. After Helene, one volunteer ran a Google Form and sheet, matching help to addresses from 7am to 11pm. https://www.foxcarolina.com/2024/11/26/spreadsheets-spreading-hope-western-north-carolina. Volunteers coordinated through Facebook groups and chats: https://www.kunc.org/2024-10-19/helene-victims-have-become-crucial-in-relief-efforts
- **Rumors:** after Helene, FEMA's Administrator called the volume of rumors "absolutely the worst I have ever seen." https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/2024/10/06/nation-world-news/us-officials-struggle-to-quash-hurricane-helene-conspiracy-theories/. Crisis-informatics research (Starbird, UW) finds that people rarely correct rumors once they spread, that fast denials from official sources slow them, and that delayed official communication speeds misinformation. https://www.nsf.gov/news/how-rumors-spread-social-media-during-weather. Validation against official sources during Harvey and Irma: *Disasters* (2023). https://www.ebsco.com/articles/earth-and-atmospheric-sciences/7654fc3c-7b03-5d39-a2c5-0097f0b13212/official-sources-news-outlets-or-search-engines-rumour-validation-on-social-media-during-hurricanes-harvey-and-irma

## 5. Why now (2025–2026)

- **Voice agents became production-grade and cheap.** OpenAI's Realtime API went GA on Aug 28, 2025, with **native SIP phone calling** and a 20% price cut ($32/$64 per 1M audio tokens in/out). https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250829-openai-gpt-realtime-. Industry estimates put voice agents at **$0.07–0.15/min vs about $7 per human-handled call**, with sub-second latency. **[WEAK: vendor blog]** https://www.usefini.com/guides/ai-voice-agents-replace-call-center-staffing-cost-per-call
- **Telephony agents are mainstream.** Twilio ConversationRelay went GA, is HIPAA-eligible, and handled about 1M calls in its first GA quarter (TWLO Q2'25). https://static0.twilio.com/en-us/blog/conversationrelay-generally-available. ElevenLabs Agents support 70+ languages with Twilio inbound numbers: https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/developers/tutorials/integrations/build-twilio-voice-elevenlabs-agents-integration
- **Generative UI got a standard.** Google open-sourced **A2UI** (Dec 15, 2025), a declarative format that lets agents emit UI rendered by native components, now at v0.9 with React/Flutter/Angular renderers. https://developers.googleblog.com/en/introducing-a2ui-an-open-project-for-agent-driven-interfaces/
- **Personal software is a consumer behavior.** "Vibe coding" was Collins Word of the Year 2025: https://www.popsci.com/technology/slop-vibe-coding-and-glazing-ai-dominates-2025s-words-of-the-year/. Lovable passed a $500M ARR run-rate with 80% non-technical builders: https://thenextweb.com/news/lovable-build-economy-500m-arr-vibe-coding
- **Sandboxed agent infrastructure exists.** Daytona offers sub-90ms sandbox creation and raised a $24M Series A led by FirstMark: https://www.streetinsider.com/PRNewswire/Daytona+Raises+%2424M+Series+A+to+Give+Every+Agent+a+Computer/25955285.html. Running generated per-family code safely is now a commodity.
- **Elders are ready for voice and AI.** AI use among adults 50+ rose from 18% to 30% in a year (AARP 2025, above).

## 6. Personas

**Ana, 46, primary caregiver (Queens).** Works full-time and has a 12-year-old. She is the 6-in-10 employed caregiver, the sandwich generation (43% of Latino caregivers), and one of the 41% who are "primary despite siblings." She gives 27+ hrs/week, and her phone is the family's medication database.

**Carmen, 79, the elder.** Spanish-first, takes 6 prescriptions (polypharmacy), and has mild memory lapses (the 22% MCI). She owns a flip phone and won't open apps. She'll say she took the blue pill when she didn't, and sometimes takes it twice. The double dose is the #1 error poison centers see.

**Marco, 43, sibling (works in Jersey City).** Wants to help but doesn't know what's happening and finds out after the crisis. He's one of the 40% of siblings who give little help, and he'd do more if the system called him when it's his turn.

**Blackout: Dee, 38, block coordinator (Canarsie).** Runs the building WhatsApp group and a Google Sheet, as mutual-aid organizers did after Helene and the LA fires. During an outage she is fielding 200 messages, half of them rumors ("cooling center on Rockaway is closed!").

**Blackout: Mrs. Alvarez, 84, 4th floor.** Lives alone, uses an oxygen concentrator, and has AC she doesn't run because of the bill. That matches the DOHMH heat-death profile (60+, Latino, chronic condition, home without AC). She isn't registered with Con Ed's LSE program, and she can still dial a phone.
