Village

Software that builds itself around your people.

One conversation about a group → a different app for each person in it → an agent that runs the group, changes the software when life changes, and refuses itself when it should.

The problem, as one person

Carmen is 78, speaks Spanish, takes a blood thinner twice a day, and will not learn an app. Her daughter Ana coordinates. Her son Marco forgets his night. Her granddaughter Lucía is far away.

Last week a stranger called Carmen as Marco and asked for money.

No app fits all four of them. The right software for each one is different, and it changes as the family's life does.

What Village does

BuildA founding conversation with Ana. Village draws each person's app from the parts of the family's life their job touches. Carmen gets three big buttons and a phone line, not an app.
RunVillage is a participant in the family chat: it asks the right person, logs what it did on its own, what it asked, and what it refused.
RebuildAfter the second money call in Marco's name, Village proposes answering unknown callers for Carmen. Ana taps yes. Every phone updates.
Any groupSame code built a second village today: a Harlem block in a heat-wave blackout, with a coordinator, volunteers and a resident on oxygen.

How it is an agent, and how it stays safe

  • Perceives events, calls, memory facts and its own Arize evals. Decides with model tool calls under three labelled modes: on its own · asked a person · refused itself.
  • Acts through tools: chat, placing and screening calls, writing memory, building, testing and releasing software.
  • Code draws the lines: the gate is pure Python, tested with the model off. Generated UI is catalog-only. Nothing ships without a sandbox build, floor tests, a phone-size browser check and evals.
  • No agent framework in between: one process, one llm() factory, every step a traced span.
